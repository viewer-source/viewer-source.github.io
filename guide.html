1. Finding a map

The list on the left holds the maps in groups; a click on a group's name opens or closes it. A small letter before a name gives the map's format: C for CellDesigner, S for SBGN-ML, none for GraphML. Search, in the row of tabs above the list, filters the list as you type and finds a name in every map.

To write: search results stepping through a map; reordering the list (development mode, Shift with a drag).

2. Moving round a map

A drag moves the map; two-finger scrolling, a pinch, or Ctrl or Cmd with the wheel zooms. The buttons over the map zoom out and in, switch between the whole map and reading size, and go full screen (with Shift: the whole page, list and tools included). The separators between the list, the map and the panel are dragged to give the map more room.

To write: Shift with the wheel to scroll the page; narrow windows and phones.

3. What a click shows

A click on a molecule, complex or reaction opens its panel: names, function, location, cross-references and the references of a reaction. A second click closes it.

To write: where the panel's data come from; Annotations on the left or right.

4. Downloads and other viewers

Downloads gives the map's files: its source (GraphML or CellDesigner), the other formats where the site has them (SBGN-ML, SBML), the picture as SVG and PNG, and the annotation table. Under them, Open in Newt or MINERVA where the map is there too. A download made while or after editing gives the map as it is on screen.

5. The right-click menu

A right click on the map gives shortcuts: switch to edit, download the source, SVG or PNG, open in Newt or MINERVA, copy the picture as PNG or SVG (for slides or a paper). Over a molecule: show its annotation, copy its name, find it in all maps. Shift with the right click gives the browser's own menu.

6. Settings

Kept in your browser, for every map:

  • Groups in the list: open all or close all.
  • Format letters: the C and S before the names, shown or hidden.
  • Map size: what 100% means, from 75 to 150%.
  • Map colours: the colours in the map's file, or the viewer's Default.
  • Source: on a map that also has an SBGN-ML file, the map's own file or that SBGN-ML (the S key flips it).
  • Association, dissociation: SBGN's circles or the generic process square.
  • Editing: Layout (moves only) or Full (a line's end may also go to another node).
  • Icons: the tabs on top of the list or in a column on the left.

8. Editing the layout

The Edit tab (the pencil, or the E key) turns the map shown into an editable one, as it is on screen. The editor is for the layout: nodes, bend points and ports moved, compartments resized, the map put on the grid, complexes packed. It adds and removes nothing in the map's content. The "?" after the tools, or the ? key, opens the full how-to.

To write: selecting; guides and snap lines; ports and visual ends; processes and their antennas; CellDesigner reactions (Make main reactant or product); SBGN-ML maps; how this differs from yEd.

9. Where edits are kept

  • With a GitHub token for the site, pasted once in the Edit window: each change is saved to the site by itself a few seconds after the last move; the site shows it about two minutes later.
  • Without a token: changes are kept in this browser only, and lost if its site data is cleared. Choose a folder on your computer (Chrome, Edge) and each change is also written there as the map's file.

To write: making the token (a recipe with every field); a conflict with a newer version on GitHub.

10. Your own maps

Where the Edit tab is, a right click on the list offers Add map and Add group: a GraphML or CellDesigner file from your computer, shown and edited as any map. A file dropped on the page is added too. Without a token it stays in this browser (and your folder); with one it goes to the site.

11. Keys
