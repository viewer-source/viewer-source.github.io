A map viewer with an edit mode: maps from yEd (GraphML), CellDesigner and SBGN-ML shown alike, each clickable for what is known about a molecule or reaction, and their layout improved in the page. This guide is an outline; sections marked to write come later. How it is built: Architecture.

1. Finding a map

The list on the left holds the maps in groups; a click on a group's name opens or closes it. Two small letters before a name give the map's format: CD for CellDesigner, SB for SBGN-ML, YE for GraphML (yEd). Search, in the row of tabs above the list, filters the list as you type and finds a name in every map.

To write: search results stepping through a map; reordering the list (development mode, Shift with a drag).

2. Moving round a map

A drag moves the map, and so do two fingers, in any direction; with Shift, two fingers move it sideways only, as a mouse wheel needs. A pinch, or Ctrl or Cmd with the wheel, zooms. On a touch screen one finger moves the map. Shift with a drag selects an area, which the right-click menu then zooms to or copies (section 5). Space held with two fingers or the wheel scrolls the page, to the text under the map. Every move and key is listed in Settings, Gestures and keys (or press ?). The buttons over the map zoom out and in, switch between the whole map and reading size, and go full screen (with Shift: the whole page, list and tools included). The separators between the list, the map and the panel are dragged to give the map more room.

To write: narrow windows and phones.

3. What a click shows

A click on a molecule, complex or reaction opens its panel: names, function, location, cross-references and the references of a reaction. A second click closes it.

To write: where the panel's data come from; Annotations on the left or right.

4. Downloads and other viewers

Downloads gives the map's files: its source (GraphML or CellDesigner), the other formats where the site has them (SBGN-ML, SBML), the picture as SVG and PNG, and the annotation table. Under them, Open in Newt or MINERVA where the map is there too. A download made while or after editing gives the map as it is on screen.

5. The right-click menu

A right click on the map gives shortcuts: switch to edit, download the source, SVG or PNG, open in Newt or MINERVA, copy the picture as PNG or SVG (for slides or a paper). Over a molecule: show its annotation, copy its name, find it in all maps. Shift with a drag outlines an area; a right click inside it zooms to it or copies that part of the picture as PNG, sharp at twice the map's own size whatever the zoom. Esc or a click on the map clears it. Shift with the right click gives the browser's own menu.

A right click on the list opens or closes all its groups at once, and hides or shows the site's example maps, the fixed set the site starts with, beside every other map. Restore example maps puts them back as the site saved them, undoing changes made to them; other maps stay as they are. It also hides or shows the file format tags, the marks before the names (CD for CellDesigner, SB for SBGN-ML, YE for GraphML).

6. Settings

Kept in your browser, for every map:

  • List: Example maps, the site's fixed starting set shown beside every other map, where the list has both. File format tags, the marks before the names (CD for CellDesigner, SB for SBGN-ML, YE for GraphML), off unless ticked, for readers who work with several formats. Both are also in the list's right-click menu.
  • Map size: what 100% means, from 75 to 150%.
  • Source: on a map that also has an SBGN-ML file, the map's own file or that SBGN-ML (the S key flips it).
  • Association, dissociation: SBGN's circles or the generic process square.
  • Editing: Layout only (moves only), or unticked, full editing (new glyphs from the palette, a line's end moved to another node). Grouped palette: the palette's signs under their SBGN group names.
  • Icons: the tabs on top of the list or in a column on the left.

8. Editing the layout

The Edit tab (the pencil, or the E key) turns the map shown into an editable one, as it is on screen. The editor is for the layout: nodes, bend points and ports moved, compartments resized, the map put on the grid, complexes packed. It adds and removes nothing in the map's content. The "?" after the tools, or the ? key, opens the full how-to.

With Layout only unticked in Settings, the palette under the tools makes new things: on an SBGN-ML map SBGN's glyphs and arcs, on a CellDesigner map CellDesigner's species, reactions, modifiers, logic gates and reduced notation, written into the CellDesigner file so it opens in CellDesigner as it was left.

To write: selecting; guides and snap lines; ports and visual ends; processes and their antennas; CellDesigner reactions (Make main reactant or product); SBGN-ML maps; how this differs from yEd.

9. Where edits are kept

  • With a GitHub token for the site, pasted once in the Edit window: each change is saved to the site's data repository by itself a few seconds after the last move, and the site's build is started; the site shows it about two minutes later. The token needs Contents read and write on the data repository and Actions read and write on the site's repository. Your own maps are saved there too, and no folder on your computer is used.
  • Without a token: changes are kept in this browser only, and lost if its site data is cleared. Choose a folder on your computer (Chrome, Edge) and each change is also written there as the map's file.

To write: making the token (a recipe with every field); a conflict with a newer version on GitHub.

10. Your own maps

Where the Edit tab is, a right click on the list offers New SBGN-ML map, New CellDesigner map, Add map from file and Add group. Either New map opens an empty map of that format at once in the Edit window, with that format's palette, named Unnamed map, in the group clicked or at the top of the list. Add map from file takes a GraphML or CellDesigner file, or an SBGN-ML file made here, from your computer, shown and edited as any map. A file dropped on the page is added too. With a token every map of yours goes to the site at once and each change is saved there. Until the site's build has drawn it, the map is shown from this browser and can be edited all the same. A map made before the token was connected goes to the site when the page next opens with it. Without a token your maps stay in this browser and in your folder. A double-click on a map of yours, or Rename map, names it in place; with a token the site's maps are renamed the same way. Groups are one level: a group holds maps, never other groups.
